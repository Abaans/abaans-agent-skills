# Examples and review cases

## Prompt-only exploration

Request: “Explore three monochrome logo directions for Northline, a fictional outdoor journal. Prompts only. Preserve the exact spelling.”

A useful response gives distinct constructions, such as a custom wordmark with an extended N terminal, a compact NL monogram, and a type-led rectangular emblem. Each has its own mechanism, legibility risk and prompt. It does not call an image generator or claim to have produced editable vectors.

Example direction:

- Thesis: a quiet editorial wordmark with one recognizable join.
- Anatomy: custom wordmark, title case, one line.
- Mechanism: the N exit stroke approaches the next letter without closing its counter.
- Exclude: mountains, compass icons and generic script lettering.
- Risk: a tight join could make the first two letters read as one.

## Focused critique

Request: “Review this concept. Keep the lettering, but the center feels cramped.”

Inspect the actual supplied image. Identify the crowded counters or joins, preserve the requested construction and propose a bounded spacing change. Do not invent a source-image appearance or restart the identity without permission.

## Reviewer checks

- A request for one direction stays one direction.
- Wrong spelling is flagged even if the image looks attractive.
- A generated raster is labeled a concept, not an editable vector or cleared trademark.
- Missing image-generation tools block generation only, not a requested research or prompt stage.
